# ENTJ

## Overview

PersonalityData.org presents ENTJs as confident and charismatic people who are inclined to take the lead and bring others together around a shared objective. The profile describes a strong drive to complete goals, a direct approach and determination. It also notes that this intensity can sometimes overwhelm people who are more reserved or sensitive, which may lead to disagreements.

## ENTJ Principles

The source characterizes ENTJs as leaders, organizers and logical decision-makers. It associates this type with planning, abstract thinking, problem-solving and taking action. The profile emphasizes motivation to achieve, high personal standards and a preference for direct communication.

The page highlights several recurring themes:

- **Drive and achievement:** A strong orientation toward goals and completing what has been started.
- **Big-picture planning:** Identifying an overall objective, forming a plan and carrying it through.
- **Leadership:** Taking responsibility, identifying problems, organizing a response and encouraging others to act.
- **Efficiency:** Looking for practical and efficient ways to complete tasks.
- **Persistence:** Continuing with determination once a goal has been selected.
- **Self-confidence:** Having a strong sense of personal capability and being relatively less affected by external criticism, according to the source.

The source also identifies possible difficulties. High standards and a strong focus on achievement may be accompanied by impatience when other people do not show the same level of motivation. A strong preference for efficiency may also lead the person to give less attention to emotional considerations during decision-making.

## The Four Letters Explained

### E — Extraversion

The source associates Extraversion with engagement in the external world, including social interaction, assertiveness and energy. The profile lists a preference for spending time with people, talking, meeting new people and maintaining a broad social network.

### N — Intuition

The source describes Intuition as a preference for noticing impressions, patterns and underlying meaning. It is associated with thinking through ideas, looking at the big picture, considering possibilities and using imagination. The page also notes that a high-level focus may sometimes make detailed or hands-on aspects less appealing.

### T — Thinking

The source associates Thinking with using facts, logic and objective criteria when making decisions. It emphasizes reason, rationality, identifying flaws and problem-solving. The page explicitly notes that a Thinking preference does not mean that a person has no emotions.

### J — Judging

The source describes Judging as a preference for structure, organization, planning and closure. Schedules, deadlines and to-do lists are presented as useful ways to organize activity and reach goals. The profile also associates this preference with decisiveness and a possible dislike of ambiguity or unexpected change.
